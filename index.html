Click one of your units to select it, then click a highlighted neighboring province to march or attack. A click orders only that unit: to attack with a stack, order each unit; clicking the province again picks the next unit there. Press Escape to clear the selection. While paused, Next day plays one day. Blue is yours, red is the Dominion. A star marks a capital. Take theirs before the campaign ends. Dominion provinces you cannot see are drawn dark: you see your provinces, the provinces next to them, and what your drone recon lights, which is outlined in green. Attacking across the river line (the blue band down the middle of the map) costs 15% of the attacking stack's attack, for both sides. Rail is supply: provinces off your rail net show hatched, and units there weaken every day. Build rail from the province card to feed new ground.

Map key: Factory N is a factory level (Fa N on phones); Fort N is walls (F N on phones, F1>2 while raising level 1 to 2); AD is air defense; Fleet is a river flotilla (Fl on phones); a dashed badge is work in progress. Unit chips read type, strength, and attack (on phones I or D and strength). The top and bottom rows are coasts: whoever holds more of a coast controls that sea, and a sea held against you cuts the steel from your coastal provinces there by 50%.

Dominion doctrine: it masses for 10 days, attacks only at clear superiority (about 1.5x your defense), and never attacks while its own units are out of supply. Starve its rail and it stalls. It always keeps a garrison of three units on its capital. It fortifies its capital, then its Spine province facing the river, whenever it holds 300 equipment. After that it builds air defense at its capital whenever it holds 400 equipment. It researches in a fixed order, each as soon as it has the points, and never changes that order for what you research. It sees by the same rule as you (its provinces and the provinces next to them), decides only on what it sees, and flies no recon. It builds no river flotilla, so its attacks across the river always pay the crossing.
