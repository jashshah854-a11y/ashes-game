The Vessari Compact vs. The Khessite Dominion

Day 1 / 150
Zoom 100%
You command the Vessari Compact, the blue nation. Win by taking Khessograd, the Dominion capital, before the campaign ends on day 150. You lose if Vesskar City, your capital, falls. If your capital still stands at the end of day 150, you win a survival victory.

The war runs in real time. At Speed 1x, one day passes about every 0.9 seconds (900 ms); at Speed 2x, about every 0.3 seconds (300 ms). Pause stops the days passing, and its button then reads Resume. The highlighted speed button is the speed in use. Orders can be given while paused or while the war runs.

Intel and fog, three states. Live: seen today, shown in full, its figures are current. Remembered: seen before but not today, dimmed and hatched on the map; its hollow plaques and its province card are from the last day you saw it, which the card names, and may have changed since. Dark: never seen; only the map itself is known, no owner, units, factories, forts, or rail are shown. Sight comes from your provinces and the provinces next to them, your units and the provinces next to them, drone wings, which see one province further (two provinces away), and an agent, which watches its own province. Fog gates information, never orders: you may still march or attack into any province you could reach before.

Map legend.

  • This is a map of the war. Blue is your nation, the Vessari Compact. Red is the Khessite Dominion.
  • A coloured band along a border shows who holds the ground on that side: blue for yours, red for the Dominion. The black line where blue meets red is the front.
  • Dashed lines are province borders. Click anywhere inside a province to select it.
  • Rivers are drawn in blue gray. Crossing a river is harder for your troops.
  • Provinces that touch the sea are coastal. Flotillas are built and sail there.
  • Railways are drawn as ties on a line. A dotted line is a railway not yet built.
  • A dash dot line at the edge of the map is the edge of the war. The land beyond it takes no part.
  • Hills and shading make the map look like real land. They are scenery only. They do not slow armies or help defenders.
  • The thin lines of latitude and longitude help you find your way. Distance in the game is counted in provinces, not degrees.
  • Blank paper is land you have never seen, and paper sea is water you have not charted. A faded brown, hatched province is what you saw last time; it may have changed.
  • The gold capital star marks a capital.
  • A gold outline marks the selected province.
  • A dashed green outline marks the provinces the selected unit can reach, its reach outline.
  • The number after Steel on a tile is that province Steel per day.
  • A Factory N badge marks a Factory level N. The badge grows with its level: level 1 is the smallest, level 2 is larger with a gold border, and level 3 is the largest and brightest, so a developed province reads apart from a starting one at a glance.
  • A Fort N badge marks a Fort level N.
  • A badge reading Fort N in M d means that fortification is being raised, a building fort, with M days left.
  • A Coast badge marks coastal or river ground a flotilla can use.
  • A Flotilla badge names the nation whose flotilla is stationed there.
  • A thick solid pale bar on a shared border is a finished railroad. No bar on a shared border means there is no railroad there.
  • A striped pale bar with a tag reading Rail N d is rail under construction with N days left.
  • A tag reading Converting N d is captured rail being converted with N days left.
  • A thicker green river bar means a flotilla supports that crossing, a supported crossing.
  • Red diagonal supply hatching over your tile means that province is off your rail supply and its units weaken.
  • A plaque on a tile marks units. The glyph shows the unit type: a soldier for infantry, a drone for a drone wing, a ship for a flotilla. The number is units times total strength, for example 2x24 is two units with 24 strength between them. A hollow dashed plaque is a last seen position and shows no numbers. A yellow alarm mark means out of supply.
  • Strength is counted in hit points, hp. A unit below full strength lowers the total strength in its plaque number, so a damaged stack reads a smaller number. The unit card shows each unit strength in hp and its attack number.
  • A plaque with a blue fill and a dark blue border is yours. A plaque with a black fill and a red border is the Dominion.
  • The selected unit plaque has a gold outline.
  • A dashed gold badge reading Order: Inf ordered here at dawn, or Order: Drone ordered here at dawn, is an order marker: it marks a province one of your units is ordered to march or attack at dawn, one badge for each ordered unit.
  • A hollow dashed plaque on a remembered tile is a unit group from the last day you saw it; the province card names that day.
  • A yellow alarm mark on a plaque means that stack is out of supply; the unit card shows its supply penalty.
  • A bright flash on a border after a day of fighting marks an attack that crossed that border that day.

Click one of your units to select it, then click a highlighted neighboring province to march or attack. Blue is yours, red is the Dominion. A star marks a capital. Take theirs before the campaign ends. Rail is supply: provinces off your rail net show hatched, and units there weaken every day. Build rail from the province card to feed new ground. An order takes effect at dawn on the next day, and a unit moves or attacks one neighboring province per day. If an enemy column marches on the province a unit stands in on the same day, that unit stays to defend its own province and its order is cancelled. The front is the border where your blue provinces meet Dominion red provinces; when a province is captured its tile changes to the captor color, so the front moves, and the war log names the province that changed hands. A unit out of supply weakens every day, and after 3 days out of supply it also loses strength to attrition.

Production

Lines build one unit at a time and drain steel every day while they work. Lines are paid before factories. If the steel stockpile cannot pay a line for a day, that line stalls and makes no progress. A finished unit deploys at your capital; a finished Interceptor does not deploy, it joins your interceptor stockpile; a finished Agent does not deploy either, it joins your agent stockpile, ready to plant from a province card; a finished Flotilla does not deploy either, it joins your flotilla stockpile, ready to station from a province card. A line that keeps building the same template gains efficiency on every funded working day and finishes units sooner, up to full efficiency at 150%. Retooling a line to a different template scraps the unit in progress, loses the steel already spent on it, and drops the line's efficiency back to its starting 100%. Queuing a unit also pays its manpower cost in full from your manpower pool, beside its steel cost; a queue attempt the pool cannot pay is refused, and the panel shows the reason.

Dominion doctrine: it masses for 10 days, attacks only at clear superiority (about 1.5x your defense), and never attacks while its own units are out of supply. Starve its rail and it stalls. Save and load

A save records the whole campaign as it stands. You can save at any time, and a save always captures a whole tick, never half of one. The browser slot keeps one save. Exporting a file keeps a copy even if the browser data is cleared. A save that cannot be read shows its reason here, and the current game keeps running.

What a save contains: the day and the seed, both nations with their provinces, units, factories, production lines and queues, research, steel, equipment and manpower, the event and texture records, the intel snapshots, and the cost exchange ledger.

Loading a save replaces the current game. The current campaign is lost unless it was saved or exported first.

War log

A capture line means the named nation took the named province and its tile changed color. A repelled or destroyed line means the defenders held and the province did not change hands. Why a battle is won: the side with the higher stack power in the fight wins, attack against defense, with supply, fortifications, river crossings, and drone effectiveness counted, the same numbers the attack preview shows before you order. Losses: a unit that loses all its strength in hit points is destroyed and no longer counts in any plaque on the map; survivors stay with lower strength, shown in the total strength on their plaque and in hit points on the unit card.
