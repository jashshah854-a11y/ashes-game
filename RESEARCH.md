# Ashes of Empires: Visual Strategy Research

**For:** Claude (Opus 5.5), as raw material for designing the image production workflow and the full visual strategy.
**Inputs:** `VISION_BOARD.md` (the adopted "Lamplit War Table" direction), the concept images in this folder, and the current simulation (70 provinces, 10x7 lattice, 900ms real-time ticks, three-state fog).
**Method:** five parallel research lanes, primary and practitioner sources, exact quotes in the lane files. Full evidence: `~/workspace/research_notes/ashes-vision-20261018/sections/lane1-map-rendering.md`, `lane2-blender-pipeline.md`, `lane3-image-workflows.md`, `lane4-rendering-tech.md`, `lane5-failure-modes.md`. Grades: STRONG / POOR / NEVER = fit for this game. Solidity tags: confirmed / supported / anecdotal / unverified.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The vision board's architecture survives the research: canvas 2D map drawn from simulation data, code-drawn plaques as the information layer, Blender miniatures as the appeal layer, image models restricted to textures and decals. Four of its five load-bearing choices are confirmed by independent evidence. But the board needs six amendments (D1 to D6 below), and the single most important correction is a reordering: **plaques and silhouette discipline are the game; miniatures are decoration on top of them.** Every documented disaster in the failure-modes lane came from letting the appeal layer carry information it could not hold at final display size. A second reordering: the board's riskiest dependency is not any rendering choice, it is process: locked render settings, worst-case-board testing, and pairwise color tests. Studios with professional QA shipped every failure catalogued here and patched them over months to years.

## The uncomfortable parts first

1. **Miniatures lose to counters on readability in both clean natural experiments.** Advance Wars Re-Boot Camp swapped 2D sprites for 3D toy models in the same game; reviewers reported tanks and medium tanks hard to tell apart, units that "blend into one another," and the loss of the GBA sprites' "inky black outlines." Panzer Corps 2's developers state the big-clear-model doctrine themselves and their publisher still had to acknowledge units are hard to recognize in 3D. HoI4, the biggest success studied, hedges: 3D models PLUS a counter chip, with NATO symbols available. The board already intuits this (its own "one ruling" puts plaques second and sprites off the list), but the build plan still spends Phase 1 effort on sprites early. Evidence says: plaque system first, silhouette test second, miniature detail last.
2. **"A fixed palette prevents color failures" is POOR as stated.** Every documented color failure happened inside a deliberately chosen palette: Age of Empires IV's orange vs red (patched 2022), its white sheep on snow biomes (patched 2025, four years in), Company of Heroes 3's warm/cool faction confusion (patched). Palettes fail by pairwise adjacency and value collision with terrain and effects, not by color count. The board's palette table is necessary and nowhere near sufficient; pairwise tests at final size, in grayscale, under the ember layer, are the actual mechanism.
3. **Atmosphere and state information cannot share pixels.** Graded NEVER on three independent cases (Civ VI fog, CoH 3 unclamped effects, AoE IV sheep). The board's vignette, lamp glow, ember effects, and texture contrast caps are all attempts to negotiate this. The research says stop negotiating: reserve a value band for state (ownership, unit marks, plaques) and hold every atmospheric layer below it, tested at map edges and under maximum effects, not at the calm center of a staged board.
4. **The Blender pipeline's biggest risk is silent look drift, not quality.** Blender 5.0 changed the compositor data model, subsurface scattering, the default volume algorithm, and view transforms. A batch rendered in November will not match a batch rendered in October unless the exact build is pinned and a golden frame is pixel-diffed at the start of every session. Factorio, the full confirmed Blender-sprite precedent, is a story about pipeline discipline (4000+ sprites, one pipeline, mask lessons learned the hard way), not about modeling talent.
5. **Hosted image models cannot hold a set together; process holds a set together.** OpenAI's gpt-image has no seed parameter at all. Gemini's character consistency is a vendor claim with a documented decay across chained edits. Midjourney's real lock is `--sref`, and its `--cref` is already dead on current versions. What works everywhere: one approved master, derive everything from the master (never from a sibling), and lock palette and value in post or in code. Ashes is unusually well positioned here precisely because its design already pushes color into code (grayscale textures, palette multiply, tint masks).
6. **There is no measured readability research in this genre's public record.** No controlled study backs any of these choices, including the ones graded STRONG. The strongest available signal is studio patch behavior: what professional teams were forced to fix after shipping. Design the workflow so Ashes generates its own measurements (timed screenshot tests: name the unit, name the owner, name the fog state) instead of trusting genre folklore, this report included.

## Decisions the research forces (amendments to the vision board)

**D1. Ownership is a gradient border band, not a fill.** HoI4 Dev Diary 11 names Ashes' exact problem and its solution: terrain mode failed because "the info you need is not clear enough," and Paradox's fix was terrain with "large fading gradient borders," political tint fading in only on zoom-out. Board amendment: ownership renders as a faction-color gradient band on the displaced shared edges (wide low-alpha stroke plus narrow brighter stroke), full-province tint near zero. This also dissolves most of the board's "textures can kill ownership" risk, because texture no longer sits under a competing fill.

**D2. Fog is texture, never darkness, and state 2 hides live numbers.** HoI4 chose cloud fog explicitly so it "isn't confused with night/shade," revealing terrain silhouettes. Songs of Conquest separates two obscurity levels cleanly. Board amendment: unexplored = cloud/void treatment; explored-but-unwatched = desaturated terrain with NO live unit numerals (ghost or suppress plaques; a numeral reads as current truth); watched = full color, live plaques. Fog states separated by value first, hue second.

**D3. Sprites ship only after passing a silhouette ID test, with a baked dark outline.** Every Blender sprite gets its outline in the render, approved as a pure silhouette at final display size (56px), in groups, from the game camera, with color removed. If two unit types cannot be told apart in grayscale silhouette, the model is wrong and no palette change fixes it. One big representative model per stack, never a cluster of small ones (Panzer Corps 2 doctrine).

**D4. The Canvas ruling stands, with nine specs added (Lane 4, section 8).** (1) Pre-rendered terrain texture drawn INTO the canvas base layer is explicitly allowed (see D5). (2) Fog edge treatment chosen now: hard per-province edges or stamped gradient sprites, NOT `ctx.filter` blur (Safari has no support; fog would silently vanish there). (3) Unit tint mechanism named: tint-mask atlas composited in code, cached per faction, never per-frame composite ops. (4) DPR capped (2 desktop, 1.5 to 2 mobile) with a per-layer pixel budget respecting iOS canvas caps (~16.7M px per canvas, ~384MB total). (5) Zoom: CSS transform during gesture, re-render on settle. (6) Hover highlight lives on the effect layer, never redraws the state layer at pointer rate. (7) Hit testing: lattice lookup plus ray casting on owned polygon geometry (headlessly testable by the build loop), not Path2D or GPU picking. (8) Reduced-motion behavior specified (the board has this; keep). (9) A named degradation order (DPR step-down, then effect density, then base texture resolution) driven by a frame-time monitor. Also: budget the effect layer as "usually running during play," since 0.9s beats at 900ms ticks mean something is almost always moving.

**D5. Pre-rendered richness goes inside the canvas, not instead of it.** Lane 4 grades the board's blanket rejection of pre-rendered maps as too broad. Fully pre-rendered as the architecture: POOR (state fights artwork, zoom ceiling, repaint on any data change). A rendered or generated terrain texture layer under the data-drawn borders, ownership bands, and fog: STRONG, the cheapest terrain richness per dev-hour available. The board's own Phase 3 (textures multiplied into fills) already does this for image-model textures; D5 extends the permission to a Blender-rendered relief/base texture if the workflow wants it, drawn into the offscreen base canvas, always subordinate to the value band rule (D-amendment in Lane 5 pattern 2: terrain texture value stays below the state band).

**D6. Readability is tested on the worst-case board, or it is not tested.** Build one fixture: all 70 provinces developed, maximum units, maximum simultaneous effects, late-game state, at default zoom, at map edges. Every visual feature, texture, and sprite batch is approved against that board with fresh-eyes timed tests (name the unit, name the owner, name the fog state). The demo board and the opening-day board are explicitly not test fixtures. This is the single highest-leverage process change in this report; density creep is the best-documented failure in the genre (Civ VI districts, Polytopia late game, HoI4 counter merging).

## Q1. Map rendering in respected strategy games (Lane 1)

**Data-drawn maps win, but only because the data is authored geography. Grade: the board's ruling STRONG, its safety condition named.** Unity of Command II's process (designer dev diaries, confirmed): GIS coastline data fitted to the hex grid, topography placed against period situation maps, rivers and rails drawn by a "randomizer" adding wiggle along hex sides then smoothed as Beziers, and a final pass dedicated to "avoid hex-shaped treelines and coastlines." The board's jitter plus midpoint displacement is the cheap procedural version of that anti-grid pass. It works because Ashes' simulation data carries rivers, coasts, and terrain. Rendering cannot invent geography the data lacks; if a future map generator emits featureless provinces, no presentation layer saves it.

**Front lines: a cased polyline on the displaced shared-edge geometry. STRONG.** HoI4's battle-plan lines and UoC2's supply presentation both draw state as bold cased lines in faction color over the terrain. Because Ashes' front is by definition a set of shared edges between opposing owners, drawing it on the already-displaced edge paths makes it inherit the anti-grid look for free. Supply: overlay toggle, not a mode (below).

**Map modes become overlay toggles on one master map. Full multi-mode UI: NEVER at this scale.** HoI4's own stated goal was a "master mapmode" because mode-switching hid information; UoC2 ships map-view buttons plus persistent unit-level alarm icons (the red "!" for out-of-supply) on the normal view. Transfer: supply overlay and front/threat overlay as one-click layers, default off, plus a persistent alarm mark for the one or two states that must never hide (out of supply is the obvious candidate).

**HD-2D's diorama feel is a fixed-camera trick; steal the cheap half.** Triangle Strategy's producers confirm the all-angles diorama is a resource sink (POOR fit). What transfers: strong value separation between terrain and units, rim-lit silhouettes, and a camera that never moves during play, all of which Ashes gets from its fixed frame and baked-outline sprites.

**Strength pips: POOR.** UoC2's step pips cap readable strength and read worse than numerals. The board's plaque numeral is strictly better; keep it.

## Q2. Blender as the sprite pipeline (Lane 2)

**Engine: Cycles for all finals, EEVEE never in the Colab path. STRONG / NEVER.** EEVEE headless on Colab has documented "unable to open a display" failures and xvfb software-GL fallbacks (a 2s frame becoming 4.5 minutes, faulty frames). A stray EEVEE-configured second scene in the file can kill a Cycles batch at composite time because the engine flag is per-scene. Whether Blender 5.2's Vulkan backend fixes headless EEVEE is UNVERIFIED; the pipeline is designed to not depend on the answer.

**The locked rig (full checklist: Lane 2, section a).** Exact build pinned (5.2.2 with hash; batch script asserts `bpy.app.version` and refuses anything else). One command entry point with `--python-exit-code 1` (load-bearing: without it, script errors exit 0 and failed batches look successful). GPU setup calls `get_devices()` after setting compute type, tries OptiX then CUDA, aborts loudly rather than silently CPU-rendering. Orthographic camera parented with key/fill/rim and shadow-catcher to one rig collection, transforms locked, re-asserted by script every run. Procedural world nodes only: zero external HDRI, texture, or font files in the render path (fonts fall back SILENTLY when paths break; that class of bug must be made impossible, not debugged). One large warm Area key from upper left, dim cool fill. Geometry Bevel modifier STRONG; Bevel shader node POOR for sprites (Cycles-only, ~20% cost, no silhouette change); subsurface scattering POOR at 256px (title render only); DOF/tilt-shift NEVER on sprites (and true focal-plane tilt does not exist in Blender; the miniature look is post Defocus on the title render only).

**Sampling and reproducibility.** Two independent practitioner sources converge: max samples 128 to 256, noise threshold 0.01, min samples 16, denoising on. Denoiser pinned to OpenImageDenoise (not OptiX) and seed fixed, for cross-GPU and cross-week reproducibility. Color management asserted every run (sRGB, AgX, locked Look, exposure 0), and the tint mask must bypass the view transform, proven by a swatch round-trip test: render a known 0.5-value mask material, read the saved pixel, confirm it survives. AgX tone-mapping a data mask is the tint pipeline's worst silent failure.

**Outputs.** Render at 512x512, downscale to 256 in a pure-Python packer using premultiplied-space resampling (the alpha-halo trap lives in the packer, not in Blender). One render yields three view layers through a File Output node: beauty, mask (white override, tintable collection only), shadow (shadow catcher). Cryptomatte POOR here (overkill, and its data does not survive the packer). A JSON manifest is born with the pixels (Factorio pattern: their spritesheeter emitted sheets plus engine code plus previews), carrying .blend hash, measured device, denoiser, frame map, trim offsets. Anchor convention: origin is ground-contact center; no auto-centering anywhere in the chain. Per-asset process isolation and skip-existing-frames so a dead Colab session resumes instead of restarting. Golden frame protocol: archived beauty/mask/shadow PNGs re-rendered and pixel-diffed at the start of every session; over-tolerance diff is a full stop.

**Precedent.** Factorio is the confirmed full-scale Blender precedent (4000+ player sprites, render layers to compositor passes). Its mask lesson transfers directly: the base sprite must be desaturated for tinting to read (the board's neutral gunmetal rule is the same discovery). Its 0.5-alpha rule is engine-specific and must NOT be copied. Dead Cells (3DS Max, lessons transfer) adds: budget for an optional per-frame Normal pass only if the game's lighting wants it (Ashes' flat code lighting does not; skip). Volumetric smoke POOR per frame; low-poly shaded smoke blobs composite better and render fast.

**Open questions that block the camera lock (for the workflow designer):** (1) the game's ground projection: true isometric (camera X 54.736°, Z 45°) vs 2:1 dimetric (X 60°, Z 45°) must match how the canvas map's provinces are drawn, or sprites will sit wrong on the ground; the vision board does not state the map's projection, because its map is top-down jittered polygons, so the sprite camera angle is a free aesthetic choice that must still be made once and locked. (2) Facing policy: rotate the model for facings vs horizontal flip (flip breaks the upper-left light rule; rotating is more renders). (3) The game's exact tint formula (multiply vs overlay on the mask) determines what gray the gunmetal base must be.

## Q3. Image model workflows for consistent asset sets (Lane 3)

**The professional pattern: one master, derive, lock in post. STRONG.** Build a master sheet (approved concept frame plus one approved texture plus a value strip) and condition every generation on it through edit endpoints. Derive each asset from the master, never from a sibling derivative (Gemini's consistency decays across chained edits; OpenAI's route is `images.edit` with `input_fidelity="high"` and the preserve list repeated on every call). The asset log (model, full prompt, reference inputs, date, post steps, gate results) is the reproducibility mechanism, because hosted models offer no other: gpt-image has no seed parameter; Midjourney seeds are within-version similarity aids only.

**For this game's set, consistency is mostly already solved by design.** Textures ship grayscale and get palette from code, so palette drift between generations is a non-problem (STRONG, and worth saying to the workflow designer: do not spend iteration budget on texture color). What remains is value and grain scale, locked in post: convert to grayscale, normalize each texture's histogram to the brief's value range, match feature scale side by side at game zoom, then admit to the repo.

**Seamless tiling: never trust the prompt.** "Seamless" in a prompt is a suggestion; no measured failure rate exists anywhere (UNVERIFIED either way), and every serious workflow includes a wrap fix. Standard: offset 50%/50%, heal the center cross, offset back; gate on a 3x3 tile test at game zoom plus a 180-degree rotation test (exposes baked directional light, which would fight the board's single light direction). Fallback for a texture that fails the heal twice: regenerate on Colab with a ComfyUI circular-padding workflow (edges match by construction; the decisive open-route advantage, and the Colab GPU is already in the pipeline). Sea and paper grain may go straight to mirror tiling in code if healing fights back.

**Decals: luminance alpha, not keying, not native transparency.** Generate scorch on plain white; alpha = 1 minus luminance, soft threshold, slight blur; gate composited over the lightest and darkest terrain with no halo and no rectangular ghost. gpt-image native transparency has documented defects (holes punched in white subjects; preview builds landing alpha at 252 to 254 instead of 255); Gemini emits no alpha at all; chroma key leaves green fringe. The two-background triangulation method (same blotch on black and on white, solve alpha per Smith and Blinn) is the fallback for a variant whose falloff misbehaves. The 9-slice UI frame leaves the alpha problem entirely: generate it as an opaque frontal rectangle and slice it in code.

**Training a LoRA for eight textures: POOR.** The open stack (LoRA + IP-Adapter + ControlNet) is the strongest consistency machinery that exists and a working practitioner still budgets a 10 to 15 percent reroll rate; for a set this small, the master-plus-post workflow reaches the same place in an afternoon instead of a week.

**Judging bake-offs well (full rubric: Lane 3, section b).** Studio practice is brief-first, bulk thumbnail selection (20 to 50 per concept), criteria ordered: silhouette/readability, value structure, brief fit, producibility. Blind review is NOT documented studio practice; Lane 3 recommends blinding model identity anyway as an adaptation, to kill model-brand bias. The biggest judging error: scoring the single best image. Score batch yield instead: fixed generation count and edit budget per model, same brief and prompt, judged composited at deployment size, with must-pass gates on brief fit and producibility (beautiful but off-brief or unbuildable loses regardless of total). Re-run a bake-off only when a model version changes, and re-blind it.

**The "describe it as a Blender build" effect: mechanism SUPPORTED, magnitude UNMEASURED.** No controlled study exists. The plausible mechanism has two parts: medium words ("3D render," "photorealistic") are documented mode switches in OpenAI's own prompting guidance, and render vocabulary selects a coherent training cluster in one move (PBR materials, studio HDRI, miniature depth of field, fixed camera), collapsing the camera and lighting variance that makes direct concept prompts wander. Confound, flagged honestly: the reframe also forces prompt completeness (materials, geometry, lighting rig all get specified), so part of the gain may be specificity rather than vocabulary. Recommendation: keep it as the house concept frame, recorded as a project finding, not a law of prompting.

## Q4. Rendering technology (Lane 4)

**Canvas 2D is right, and it is not close. STRONG.** The scale math: Ashes' worst case is roughly 140 path operations per tick at 1.1 Hz and about 200 sprites per animated frame. A 2013 integrated GPU (Intel HD4000) already rendered 1,600 sprites at 60 FPS in published benchmarks. Canvas 2D's real failure mode is per-call CPU cost, and this scene's call counts are one to two orders of magnitude below every measured breaking point. The closest shipping comparable, OpenFront, ran a layered canvas architecture structurally identical to the board's ruling and scaled to a claimed one million monthly players on it; its later WebGL migration signals were driven by a per-pixel territory grid with hundreds of concurrent units, a different workload, not a bigger version of 70 polygons. Citing OpenFront for WebGL at Ashes' scale is a category error.

**WebGL: raw POOR, PixiJS POOR now and the designated escape hatch.** Its real wins (batched sprites at ~100k scale, shader fog, post effects) are all irrelevant at 200 sprites. Its real costs are paid immediately: a context-loss rebuild class, text that ends up in the DOM anyway, no help with picking, and shader debugging burden on an AI build loop that cannot see the screen. Re-grade triggers, named now so the decision does not get relitigated by vibes: sustained counts past roughly 1,000 concurrent sprites, a move to per-pixel territory, or core gameplay depending on shader post-effects.

**Hard traps, sourced (full list: Lane 4, section 9).** `shadowBlur` is "very expensive" (web.dev) and is banned from the frame path. `ctx.filter` has NO Safari support at all: any fog or glow built on it silently vanishes for Safari users. No per-frame gradients, no `getImageData` in the frame path, no per-frame `fillText` (the DOM text ruling is independently confirmed right on performance, zoom crispness, and accessibility grounds). Pre-render every static layer to offscreen canvases; the state layer redraws on change, never on a timer; rAF runs only while animation is live.

**Honest gap:** no published benchmark measures this exact scene. The canvas conclusion is strong inference from call-count architecture plus sprite benchmarks. A one-afternoon throwaway benchmark (the worst-case board, D6, in headless Chromium with frame timing) closes it, and the build plan already contains the harness (headless screenshot checks) to do it.

## Q5. Failure modes (Lane 5)

The full case file is Lane 5 (nine cases, quotes, patch notes). The five patterns most likely to bite Ashes, each with its early-warning sign, are D3, D6, and the value-band rule above, plus:

**Pattern: faction adjacency failure appears in motion and at small size first.** AoE IV's orange/red and CoH 3's warm/cool confusion were invisible in faction-select screens and obvious in play. The board already worries about ember vs Khessite red; the research generalizes it: test the FULL palette pairwise at whole-map size, in grayscale, and under the ember glow layer, and schedule one deliberate team-color tuning pass after the first full sprite batch, because the documented base rate is that the first pass overshoots. Separate factions by value and a second channel (plaque shape, edge pattern), never hue alone; the board's plaques already provide the second channel, which is another reason they outrank the miniatures.

**Pattern: studios adapt to taste complaints and patch information complaints.** HoI4 and Civ VI both show audiences adapting to a style they first hated. The operational rule for reading Ashes' own future feedback: sort complaints into taste (adaptable, discount) and information (unit identity, owner, fog state: patch-bound, never discount). "Players will adapt" applied to an information failure is how the documented failures shipped.

**Folklore check, so the workflow does not design against ghosts:** the claim that most HoI4 players use NATO counters is UNVERIFIED (no usage data exists; at least one major mod recommends counters off). Advance Wars Re-Boot Camp's art hurting its sales is UNVERIFIED (reviews split; some praised the toy look). Civ VI's "apology patch" for its art never happened; the 2019 Civ V look was an art director's mod, not a Firaxis patch.

## Consolidated workflow implications (for the design pass)

1. Order of production: plaque system and worst-case test board first; map geometry and gradient ownership second; Blender rig lock and golden frame third; textures via the master-sheet workflow fourth; sprites last, gated by the silhouette test.
2. Every pipeline (Blender, image models, canvas) gets the same shape: a locked spec written before generation, a deterministic post step that does not trust the generator, an acceptance gate with a named test, and a log that makes the batch reproducible.
3. The two numbers the project should start measuring on itself: timed fresh-eyes identification (unit, owner, fog state) on the worst-case board, and per-batch sprite diff against the golden frame. Both are cheap; both replace genre folklore with local evidence within one build cycle.

## Open unknowns (consolidated, all flagged in the lanes)

- The game's ground projection and facing policy (blocks the Blender camera lock; a decision, not a research gap).
- Real per-frame render times on the lowest accepted Colab GPU tier (unmeasured; affects title render scheduling, not sprites).
- Whether Blender 5.2's Vulkan backend makes EEVEE viable headless (unverified; pipeline designed to not need it).
- Gemini image successor behavior and the current status of gpt-image transparency defects (moving targets; re-check at workflow design time).
- HoI4's exact counter-merging thresholds (documented only through player observation).
